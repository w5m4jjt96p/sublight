## Description (4000)
Nothing you see out there is happening now.

Sublight is a live map of the small fleet of robotic spacecraft still working across the solar system, placed by the one number that never lies about distance: how long their signal takes to reach Earth. The light you see from Perseverance is about 15 minutes old. From Voyager 1, almost a day.

• A real-time map of the Sun, the planets, the Moon, and the active robotic fleet, drifting along their orbits at true speed.
• Tap any spacecraft for its current one-way and round-trip light time, its distance from Earth, and its story.
• Rovers include a surface traverse: their real driven path over NASA satellite imagery of Mars, where tapping any stop loads that day's raw frames.
• A gallery of the newest images the fleet has sent home, each as old as its journey here.
• Optional alerts when a rover or DSCOVR sends home new imagery.

Everything is measured, never invented. Positions come from NASA/JPL Horizons ephemerides, and imagery from NASA missions and the NASA Image Library, refreshed daily. If a value is unknown, Sublight shows a dash rather than a guess.

Free, with no ads and no tracking. Sublight is an independent project and is not affiliated with or endorsed by NASA or any space agency.
